Surcharging

Know the rules. Then let Sola keep them.

A surcharge is a percentage added to a credit card sale to cover what the merchant pays to accept cards. Card brands and states decide when it's allowed and how much. Here's what they require, what it costs to get it wrong, and what to offer where surcharging isn't an option.

The rules

Card brands set the baseline. States can lower the cap or ban surcharging outright. Here's each rule, and what Sola does about it.

What counts as a surcharge

  • A percentage of the sale, never a flat fee
  • Only on the sale: not tax or tip
  • Brand neutral: the same rate on every card brand

Sola: if a merchant's rates differ by brand, the lowest one sets the surcharge for all of them.

Which cards

  • Credit cards only
  • Not debit, even when it's run as credit
  • Not prepaid or gift cards

Sola: reads every card's BIN and leaves the fee off debit and prepaid. The sale still goes through.

How much

  • Up to 3% under card brand rules
  • Less where a state caps it: 2% in Colorado
  • Never more than the merchant pays to accept the card

Sola: caps the rate at enrollment at the lowest of the three, so it can't be set too high.

Where

  • Card present: the merchant's state decides
  • Card not present: the cardholder's billing state decides
  • Not permitted in CA, CT, MA, ME, and Puerto Rico

Sola: checks the merchant's state at enrollment and the cardholder's billing state on every online sale.

Disclosure

  • Signs at the entrance and the point of sale
  • On screen before the customer pays
  • Its own line on the receipt

Sola: Sola Terminal shows the fee before payment and prints it on its own line. Signs are up to the merchant.

Refunds

  • Full refund: the surcharge goes back too
  • Partial refund: a matching share of it goes back

Sola: refunds the surcharge with the sale, in proportion on a partial refund.

A fee at the register isn't a surcharge program.

Secret shoppers

Card brands run mystery shopper checks on signs, rates, and receipts. Any sale could be the test.

Fines

Fines go through the processor to the merchant, and they climb until the problem is fixed.

Processing shut down

A merchant who keeps surcharging the wrong way can lose the ability to accept cards at all.

Disputes and lawsuits

Customers dispute fees they shouldn't have paid, and states and class actions go after improper card fees.

Can't surcharge? Other ways to cover the cost.

Merchants in California, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Maine, and Puerto Rico can't surcharge. They can still stop absorbing the cost of cards.

Cash discount

The posted price is the card price. Customers who pay cash get a discount at the register. A discount for cash isn't a fee on cards.
